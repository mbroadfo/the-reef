# Hunter on real historical prices (offline replay)

> **Read this first.** This is a *fixed-universe, survivorship-biased* replay. It is **not** a survivorship-free historical simulation and it is **not evidence of an investment edge.** See Limitations.

Generated by `tools/historical_replay.py` from a local cache of free Yahoo Finance daily bars (2015-01-02 .. 2026-10-30). Aggregate results only; the vendor rows are never committed. Hunter v1 rules, D0 sleeve weight 40% ($4,000 of a $10,000 fund), the real Risk Engine and the same `advance()` loop as the synthetic walkthrough; each window is a fresh epoch. No LLM, no Atlas.

Returns are **price returns** on the sleeve's funded capital ($4,000); dividends are excluded for Hunter, SPY and the basket alike. SPY is bought at the first session's open with whole shares and held. *Univ. buy&hold* is an equal-dollar basket of the same survivor universe: a yardstick for how much the universe itself flatters a long-only strategy, not a tradable benchmark.

**Paused** = share of sessions the fund sat 10%+ below its high-water mark, where the Risk Engine blocks new entries until a human resumes (provisional limit). The replay has no human, so in the continuous run the pause never lifts and Hunter stops trading for years; that, not strategy behaviour, explains its low exposure there. The single-year windows restart each epoch at a fresh high-water mark and are the fairer read.

## Limitations (these change how to read every number above)

1. **Survivorship bias.** The universe is a fixed list of large US companies that are listed *today*. Names that were delisted, acquired, bankrupt or dropped from the large-cap set during 2016-2026 are absent, and the free source does not carry them. Every long-only result is biased upward by an unknown amount. The *Univ. buy&hold* column shows how flattering the universe is to a passive holder; it is not a measurement of Hunter's own bias.
2. **Selection hindsight.** Choosing today's large caps for 2016 includes winners nobody could have picked then, and sectors are today's classification applied to all history.
3. **Corporate actions.** Prices are split-adjusted to today's share counts, so historical share counts and whole-share rounding differ from what could have been traded then. Dividends are not credited (this understates SPY and the basket more than Hunter, which holds briefly). Mergers, spin-offs and cash acquisitions of held names cannot occur because only survivors are present; the engine's cash-out path is untested on real data.
4. **Execution.** Fills are at the daily open or the stop price plus the stated frictions. Daily bars cannot show whether a stop was hit before or after another intraday event, book depth, or halts. Real opening gaps in volatile names can be worse than the stress case.
5. **Free data quality.** Yahoo data is unaudited and may be revised; the cache is a snapshot. Its terms do not allow redistribution, so this report publishes aggregates only and the replay cannot run in CI.
6. **Statistics.** Ten annual windows of one strategy with hand-picked parameters. There is no out-of-sample test and no parameter search was run. Nothing here supports a claim of edge.
